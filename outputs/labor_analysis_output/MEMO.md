# Foxconn Zhengzhou labor data — findings memo

Generated by `scripts/labor_data.py` from the existing workbooks listed in `docs/00_文件说明.md`.
No source file was modified; all output lives in this folder (`tables/`, `charts/`).
Re-run with `python3 scripts/labor_data.py` from the repo root to regenerate everything.

Evidence strength key: **strong** = official/administrative record or a figure
corroborated across independent sources; **medium** = a single credible source
(NGO field survey, news, environmental filing) with plausible but unverified
figures; **weak** = a modeled/derived estimate, small sample, or figures that
conflict across sources.

## A. Workforce size and composition

### A1 — Insured workers vs. total workforce, 2016–2025
**Strong** for the insured-worker series itself (official annual reports, entity
by entity); **medium** for how it relates to actual total headcount.

- All four entities' *insured* (regular, direct-hire) headcount fell sharply and
  continuously: 鸿富锦 213,182→33,498 (2016→2025), 富联精密/富泰华 23,052→8,617,
  富联裕展/河南裕展 9,000→12,784 (peaked ~51,000 in the 2016 filing), 河南富驰
  essentially flat at a few thousand.
- CLW/news point estimates of *total on-site workforce* (which includes
  dispatch/student workers not captured by insurance filings) show the familiar
  seasonal swing: peak >300,000 (2017–18, 2023), trough ~60,000–70,000
  (2018, 2022), 2025 peak 150,000–200,000. These are field-survey observations,
  not a census, and are not on the same measurement cadence as the annual
  insured-worker filings — see `charts/A1_insured_workers_vs_total_workforce.png`.
- Environmental-approval ("EIA") design headcounts are a *ceiling companies
  commit to in permit filings*, not measured employment. 富泰华's stayed
  boilerplate at 23,000 across 2012–2022 filings; 富联裕展/河南裕展's fell from
  51,000 (2020) → 30,000 → 26,000 → 11,614 (2023), each drop explicitly
  attributed in the filing text to automation upgrades — see
  `charts/A1b_yuzhan_design_headcount_decline.png`. This is the single cleanest
  piece of evidence in this dataset that automation investment is displacing
  planned headcount at a specific factory.
- Limitation: there is no single continuous "total headcount" series for any
  entity across the period — only these three incompatible point-in-time
  measures (insured / CLW field estimate / EIA design cap), which is exactly the
  caveat flagged in `docs/LABOR_DATA.md`.

### A2 — Dispatch worker share
**Medium** for the CLW-reported percentages themselves; **weak** for the
"insured vs. total" proxy estimates built from them.

- CLW reports dispatch share at 55% (2018), ~50% (2019), and >50% (2025, with an
  explicit 2025 breakdown: ~60,000–80,000 regular vs. ~80,000–110,000 dispatch
  at peak) — all far above the 10% legal cap under the Labor Contract Law
  (`charts/A2_dispatch_share_vs_legal_cap.png`).
- The 2025 regular-worker estimate (60,000–80,000) sits close to that year's
  insured-worker total (61,825) — a **medium-strength** cross-check that the two
  sources roughly agree on the *direct-hire* population even though they were
  collected completely independently (`charts/A2_insured_vs_CLW_2025_snapshot.png`).
- Two "proxy" methods for estimating dispatch headcount from insured workers are
  in `tables/A2_dispatch_estimate_vs_insured_PROXY.csv`, both explicitly labeled
  as modeled, not measured. The 2018 row is flagged **unusable**: CLW's 2018
  figure (~60,000) is an explicitly off-season trough observation, while the
  insured filing for that year (190,666) is a year-end snapshot — combining them
  produces an implausible >400,000 "total workforce," which is kept in the table
  only for transparency. The 2019 and 2025 rows are more defensible, though at
  exactly 50% share the "dispatch = total − insured" arithmetic is tautological.
- Separately, the six dispatch/staffing agencies identified (安徽天造, 河南信广达,
  江苏思远, 郑州新力众, 河南群英会, 河南启宏) each insure only single digits to a
  few hundred workers a year (max 340, in 2019) — nowhere near the tens of
  thousands of dispatch workers CLW describes. This is **strong** evidence that
  dispatch workers are largely uninsured/off the books at the agency level too,
  not just undercounted at Foxconn's own entities
  (`tables/A2_dispatch_agency_insured_headcounts.csv`).

### A3 — What Revelio can and cannot represent
**Strong** conclusion (the data source's own internal pattern makes the case).

- Revelio's estimated Zhengzhou (Hon Hai) headcount is essentially flat,
  ~6,600–8,100, across 2008–2025 — it does not move during the 2022 COVID
  lockdown crisis or any peak-season hiring surge, which is itself evidence the
  series is mostly interpolated/backfilled rather than a real annual count.
  Coverage against the actual workforce is roughly 8,100 / 150,000–300,000 ≈
  3–5% (`charts/A3_revelio_coverage_vs_reality.png`).
- The June 2025 role breakdown skews toward office/technical categories
  ("unknown," Technician, Engineer, Operations, Service Worker); frontline
  assembly, dispatch, and student workers are essentially invisible in this
  source (`charts/A3_revelio_role_composition_2025.png`). Use Revelio only to
  illustrate what LinkedIn-style professional-network data can and cannot see —
  never as a workforce-size estimate.

## B. Pay

### B1 — Regular vs. dispatch pay calculation
**Medium-strong**: the underlying structure is directly sourced from CLW's 2023
and 2025 field reports and a real court judgment; the specific rates are
CLW-quoted defaults, not a full payroll audit.

- `tables/B1_pay_structure_by_worker_type.csv` lays out how regular workers,
  rebate-type dispatch, hourly-type dispatch, and student workers differ on
  base pay, overtime rules, social insurance, sick leave, and what a worker
  loses by leaving early.
- Applying CLW's own default parameters, a **same-hours** comparison shows
  hourly-type dispatch workers earning a higher *pretax* monthly figure than
  regular workers in every season scenario (1.53×–1.74×,
  `charts/B1_dispatch_regular_pretax_ratio.png`) — but this reverses in
  practical terms: dispatch pay has no overtime multiplier baked in for a
  higher base, no social insurance, and roughly half of the hourly rate (the
  "差价"/price-gap component) is deferred to the following month and forfeited
  entirely if the worker leaves before the 25th, or leaves within 90 days for
  rebate-type contracts (`charts/B1_income_scenarios_regular_vs_dispatch.png`).
  The pretax number alone therefore overstates how much better off dispatch
  workers are.

### B2 — White-collar vs. factory-worker pay
**Weak-medium**, and the two series are not on a comparable footing.

- White-collar figures are Revelio-posted (non-model-predicted) salaries only,
  converted from annual RMB to RMB/month; median runs roughly ¥7,000–10,000 in
  years with adequate sample size. **Data-quality finding**: the 2026 posting
  batch has annual salary values as low as ~¥1,000 (implying ~¥90/month), almost
  certainly a unit/parsing artifact — flagged and excluded from the reliable
  series rather than silently averaged in
  (`tables/B2_whitecollar_monthly_pay_by_year.csv`). 2022's unusually low median
  (¥3,391) traces to a handful of postings for roles like "delivery driver" and
  "lifeguard" mixed into the sample — a reminder that Revelio's occupational
  tagging for Zhengzhou is noisy.
- Factory-worker figures come from two sources that don't line up in time or
  method: (a) posted base salary (no overtime) from the recruitment-ad panel,
  and (b) a *modeled* monthly income (hourly-wage median × 226 "typical"
  monthly hours — an assumption, not a payslip), cross-checked against CLW's
  own directly observed monthly income (¥2,000–5,100 depending on season/skill
  level). See `charts/B2_whitecollar_vs_factory_pay.png` and
  `tables/B2_whitecollar_vs_factory_comparison.csv`.
- Headline: white-collar posted pay is roughly 2–3× factory pay, but the
  comparison is base-salary-ask vs. overtime-inclusive-earnings, so the true
  gap is smaller than the raw numbers suggest, and could go either way once
  factory overtime is fully counted against realistic white-collar total comp
  (which Revelio doesn't capture at all, since it uses posted base only).

## C. Recruitment posts (fskzpw.com, 2010–2026)

### C1 — Recruitment post types over time
**Medium-strong**: classification rules are auditable and every dispatch-related
claim is backed by a quoted source snippet, but sample sizes are small in most
years and category boundaries (e.g., "direct-hire marketing language" vs. an
undisclosed dispatch arrangement) are inherently fuzzy.

- Classification logic (see `scripts/labor_data.py`, section C1): a post is
  "hourly-type dispatch" if it carries an extracted hourly wage field,
  "rebate-type dispatch" if it carries a rebate/signing-bonus field (and no
  hourly field), "student/summer" or "short-term" if the *title or summary*
  (always usable) contains matching keywords, else "regular/unspecified."
  Per `docs/LABOR_DATA.md`'s contamination rule, body text is used for keyword
  search **only** for posts not flagged `正文含后期模板` (i.e., where the site
  has not overwritten the body with a later FAQ template) — 313 of 449 posts
  are flagged contaminated and fall back to title+summary only.
- Result: essentially 100% "regular/unspecified" through 2018 (no public price
  fields at all before 2019, matching the source file's own note), then a rapid
  shift — by 2025–2026, ~85–95% of posts are explicitly rebate- or hourly-type
  dispatch ads (`charts/C1_post_type_mix_over_time.png`; raw counts in
  `charts/C1_post_type_counts_over_time.png`, since several years have fewer
  than 30 posts and the share chart alone can be misleading for those).
- 149 posts explicitly mention dispatch-indicating language (派遣, 同工同酬,
  差价, 返费协议, etc.), with quotes and source links in
  `tables/C1_dispatch_and_related_language_evidence_quotes.csv`. Notably, this
  includes *uncontaminated* 2021–2022 posts explicitly describing "新/老派遣工"
  distinctions and rebate schedules — direct evidence dispatch hiring was
  already established well before the 2025 CLW report, from the agency's own
  contemporaneous language. 171 posts separately use direct-hire marketing
  language ("直招"/"官方直招"/"正式工"), which per the known site issue may just
  be recruiting-agency marketing rather than proof of non-dispatch status — do
  not treat this count as a "regular hiring" measure.

### C2 — Recruitment trends vs. seasonality, insurance, third-party data
**Medium**: the qualitative direction is consistent across three independent
signals, but recruitment-ad volume is a small, noisy sample and correlation is
not established statistically.

- Monthly rebate and hourly-wage levels rise through the 2021–2022 automation
  push (see A1b), pull back in 2023 as 富联裕展's design headcount was cut, then
  rise again in the 2024–2025 hiring seasons — consistent with CLW's
  description of rebates/wages spiking in peak season
  (`charts/C2_monthly_recruitment_intensity_and_pricing.png`).
- Indexed to a common 2019 base, the annual share of dispatch-type recruitment
  ads rises sharply (to ~250–580% of its 2019 level) while insured-worker
  headcount falls steadily (to ~60% of 2019) over the same years
  (`charts/C2_dispatch_post_share_vs_insured_decline.png`) — directionally
  consistent with CLW's own qualitative note that "before 2016 new workers were
  mostly hired directly by the factory; now most are supplied by dispatch
  companies." This is presented as a directional, indexed comparison
  specifically to avoid a dual-axis chart, not as a measured correlation.

## Known cross-cutting limitations

1. **No single consistent headcount denominator.** Insured workers, CLW field
   estimates, EIA design headcounts, and Revelio counts are four different
   measures with different populations, timing, and collection methods. Every
   chart comparing them says so directly in its footnote; do not let a
   dashboard silently imply they're the same series.
2. **Small samples throughout.** Most years of recruitment-post data have fewer
   than 30–50 posts; most CLW figures come from field interviews of roughly
   100 workers. Year-over-year swings can be sampling noise, not real change.
3. **Recruitment posts cannot size the workforce.** Per `docs/LABOR_DATA.md`, C1/C2
   describe *hiring language and pricing*, never used here to estimate the
   dispatch share of total employment (that's A2's job, using CLW figures).
4. **Some figures could not be fully reconciled** (e.g., A2's 2018 proxy row)
   and are kept in the output, explicitly flagged, rather than silently
   dropped or smoothed over.
